## MEDDPICC Framework

### Overview

MEDDPICC is an enterprise deal qualification framework used to assess whether an opportunity is real, winnable, and worth pursuing. Each element represents a critical factor in the buying decision.

### The Framework

| Element | Question | What to Validate |
|---------|----------|------------------|
| **M**etrics | What is the measurable impact of solving this problem? | Quantified business outcome the buyer cares about |
| **E**conomic Buyer | Who has the authority and budget to approve this purchase? | Access to the person who can say yes (not just the evaluator) |
| **D**ecision Criteria | What criteria will they use to evaluate solutions? | Formal or informal scoring criteria, must-haves vs. nice-to-haves |
| **D**ecision Process | What is the process they will follow to make a decision? | Steps, stakeholders involved, timeline, procurement requirements |
| **P**aper Process | What is the legal/procurement process to get a contract signed? | Legal review, security review, procurement, contract terms |
| **I**dentified Pain | What is the specific pain driving this initiative? | Stated pain (what they say) vs. root cause (why it matters) |
| **C**hampion | Who inside the organization is advocating for us? | Someone with power, influence, and a personal reason to act |
| **C**ompetition | Who else is being considered and what is our competitive position? | Direct competitors, status quo, alternative approaches |

### MEDDPICC Scoring

| Score | Description | Action |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| **3 - Validated** | Confirmed directly with the customer, evidence documented | Proceed with confidence |
| **2 - Developing** | Some information gathered, needs further validation | Plan specific discovery actions |
| **1 - Identified** | Aware of the element but not yet validated | Prioritize in next customer interaction |
| **0 - Unknown** | No information on this element | Critical gap — must address immediately |

### Champion Testing

A true champion has three qualities — test for all three:

| Quality | Test | What to Look For |
|---------|------|------------------|
| **Power** | Can they influence or make the decision? | Title, role in decision process, organizational authority |
| **Influence** | Do others listen to and follow their recommendations? | Track record of internal advocacy, peer respect |
| **Personal Win** | Do they personally benefit from choosing us? | Career advancement, risk reduction, visibility, solving their specific problem |

### Champion Enablement

Once identified, enable your champion with:
- **Internal business case** they can present to their leadership
- **ROI data** specific to their organization's metrics
- **Competitive differentiation** talking points for internal discussions
- **Risk mitigation** arguments to address objections they will face
- **Reference customers** they can contact independently

## SPIN Selling Framework

### Overview

SPIN Selling is a consultative questioning methodology that guides prospects from awareness of their situation to recognition of the need and value of a solution.

### Question Types (In Order)

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **S**ituation | Understand current state and context | "How are you currently handling [process]?" |
| **P**roblem | Uncover difficulties, dissatisfaction, or challenges | "What challenges do you face with [current approach]?" |
| **I**mplication | Explore the consequences of the problem remaining unsolved | "What happens to [metric] when [problem] occurs?" |
| **N**eed-Payoff | Have the prospect articulate the value of solving the problem | "If you could [solve problem], what impact would that have on [metric]?" |

### SPIN Progression

```
Situation → Understand their world
  Problem → Surface pain they may not have articulated
    Implication → Amplify urgency by connecting pain to business impact
      Need-Payoff → Prospect states the value of solving (they sell themselves)
```

### Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Fails | Better Approach |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| Too many Situation questions | Feels like an interrogation, wastes time | Research before the call, limit to 3-5 |
| Jumping to Need-Payoff | Prospect has not felt the pain deeply enough | Let Implication questions build urgency naturally |
| Leading questions | Prospect feels manipulated, not understood | Genuinely curious questions, follow the thread |
| Skipping Implication | Problem stays abstract, no urgency | Connect every problem to a measurable business impact |

## The Challenger Sale Framework

### Overview

The Challenger Sale is a selling methodology based on the insight that the best salespeople teach customers something new about their business, tailor their message to the stakeholder, and take control of the sale.

### The Three T's

| Behavior | Description | Application |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| **Teach** | Share insights the customer did not have | Industry benchmarks, peer comparisons, unexpected data, new problem framing |
| **Tailor** | Customize the message to the individual stakeholder | Different value message for CFO vs. CTO vs. end user |
| **Take Control** | Guide the buying process assertively but respectfully | Push back on unreasonable demands, propose next steps, set timelines |

### Challenger Conversation Flow

1. **Warmer** — Demonstrate understanding of their world (credibility)
2. **Reframe** — Introduce a surprising insight that challenges their current thinking
3. **Rational Drowning** — Show the magnitude of the problem with data
4. **Emotional Impact** — Connect the data to a personal or organizational story
5. **New Way** — Present the solution framework (not your product yet)
6. **Your Solution** — Map your product to the framework you just built

### Teaching Pitch Structure

```
"Most companies in your space believe [conventional wisdom].
But our data shows [surprising insight that challenges this].
This means [implication for their business].
The companies getting ahead are [new approach].
Here's how that works: [solution framework].
And this is where we fit: [your product mapped to framework]."
```

## Command of the Message

### Overview

Command of the Message is a value messaging framework that ensures every sales conversation anchors on customer value, differentiation, and defensible proof points.

### Value Framework

| Element | Definition | Example Structure |
|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Required Capabilities** | What the customer needs the solution to do | "You need a solution that can [capability 1], [capability 2], and [capability 3]" |
| **Positive Business Outcomes** | Measurable results from using the solution | "This will enable you to [outcome], resulting in [metric improvement]" |
| **Negative Consequences** | What happens if they do nothing | "Without addressing this, you risk [consequence 1] and [consequence 2]" |
| **Differentiators** | What makes you uniquely able to deliver | "Only we can [unique capability] because [proof point]" |

### Before/After Framework

| Dimension | Before (Current State) | After (Desired State) |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| **Process** | [How they do it today] | [How they will do it with us] |
| **Metrics** | [Current performance] | [Expected improvement] |
| **Risk** | [Current exposure] | [Mitigated risk] |
| **Cost** | [Cost of current approach] | [Reduced cost / better ROI] |

## BANT Framework

### Overview

BANT is a basic lead qualification framework. While less sophisticated than MEDDPICC for enterprise deals, it is useful for initial qualification and SMB/mid-market sales.

| Element | Question | Qualification Criteria |
|---------|----------|----------------------|
| **B**udget | Do they have budget allocated? | Budget confirmed or budget process identified |
| **A**uthority | Are we talking to the decision maker? | Direct authority or clear path to decision maker |
| **N**eed | Do they have a genuine problem we solve? | Stated need aligned with our solution |
| **T**imeline | When do they need a solution? | Active timeline (not "someday") |

### BANT Scoring

| Score | Criteria Met | Action |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| **4/4** | All four confirmed | High priority, accelerate |
| **3/4** | Three confirmed | Active opportunity, work the gap |
| **2/4** | Two confirmed | Qualifying, needs more discovery |
| **1/4** | One confirmed | Early stage, nurture |
| **0/4** | None confirmed | Not qualified, do not invest |

### When to Use BANT vs. MEDDPICC

| Context | Framework |
|---------|-----------|
| SMB / self-serve | BANT |
| Mid-market transactional | BANT + Champion |
| Enterprise complex | MEDDPICC |
| Multi-stakeholder | MEDDPICC |
| Long sales cycle (6+ months) | MEDDPICC |

## Objection Handling Framework

### The LAER Model

| Step | Action | Example |
|------|--------|---------|
| **L**isten | Let the prospect fully state their objection | Do not interrupt, take notes, acknowledge |
| **A**cknowledge | Validate their concern (not agree, validate) | "I understand why that is a concern..." |
| **E**xplore | Ask questions to understand the root cause | "Help me understand — is the concern about [X] or [Y]?" |
| **R**espond | Address the root cause, not the surface objection | Tailored response based on what you learned |

### Common B2B Objections

| Objection | Root Cause | Response Pattern |
|-----------|-----------|------------------|
| "Too expensive" | Value not established | Reframe to ROI and cost of status quo |
| "We're happy with current solution" | No compelling event or pain | Introduce insight about hidden cost or risk |
| "Not a priority right now" | Urgency not established | Connect to an existing priority, quantify cost of delay |
| "Need to talk to my team" | Not the decision maker or not sold yet | Offer to present to the team together |
| "We tried something similar and it failed" | Risk aversion | Understand what failed, differentiate, offer proof (POC) |
| "We can build this ourselves" | Underestimating effort | Total cost of ownership vs. buy, opportunity cost |

## Sales Cycle Stage Definitions

### Standard B2B SaaS Stages

| Stage | Definition | Exit Criteria | Evidence Required |
|-------|-----------|---------------|-------------------|
| **1. Prospect** | Identified as potential fit | Meeting accepted | ICP match confirmed |
| **2. Discovery** | Active engagement, understanding needs | Pain identified and quantified | SPIN discovery notes, problem statement |
| **3. Solution** | Presenting tailored solution | Solution fit confirmed | Demo completed, POC if needed |
| **4. Evaluation** | Technical and business evaluation | Evaluation criteria met | Technical win, champion confirmed |
| **5. Negotiation** | Commercial terms discussion | Terms agreed | Proposal accepted, pricing approved |
| **6. Closing** | Contract and procurement | Signature | Signed contract, PO received |
| **7. Won** | Deal closed | — | Revenue recognized |
| **Lost** | Deal lost at any stage | — | Win/loss debrief completed |

### Stage Velocity Benchmarks

| Metric | SMB | Mid-Market | Enterprise |
|--------|-----|-----------|-----------|
| Average cycle length | 14-30 days | 30-90 days | 90-270 days |
| Discovery to Solution | 3-7 days | 7-21 days | 21-60 days |
| Evaluation duration | 3-7 days | 14-30 days | 30-90 days |
| Negotiation to Close | 3-14 days | 14-30 days | 30-60 days |

## Multi-Threading Strategy

### Stakeholder Mapping

| Role | Motivation | Engagement Strategy |
|------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Economic Buyer** | ROI, business outcomes, risk | Executive briefing, ROI case, peer references |
| **Technical Buyer** | Feasibility, integration, security | Technical deep dive, POC, architecture review |
| **User Buyer** | Ease of use, workflow improvement | Hands-on demo, trial, user testimonials |
| **Coach/Champion** | Personal win, career impact | Enable with internal pitch materials, make them the hero |
| **Blocker** | Risk, change resistance, competing priority | Understand concerns, address directly, find common ground |

### Threading Targets

| Deal Size | Minimum Threads | Ideal Threads |
|-----------|----------------|---------------|
| SMB | 1-2 | 2-3 |
| Mid-Market | 2-3 | 3-5 |
| Enterprise | 3-5 | 5-8 |

> Added 2026-06-24. The qualification frameworks above (MEDDPICC, SPIN, Challenger, Command of the Message, BANT, LAER) are **unchanged**. What changed in 2026 is *how* the methodology gets applied: AI now extracts deal evidence and pre-scores qualification fields. This section is additive — it does not modify any framework definition above.

### What AI Changed (and What It Did Not)

| Dimension | Pre-AI Practice | 2026 AI-Assisted Practice | Unchanged Core |
|-----------|-----------------|---------------------------|----------------|
| **Deal coaching** | Manager coaches on the rep's *narrative* of the deal | Manager coaches on AI-extracted **call/deal behavior** — what was actually said, who attended, which MEDDPICC elements went unaddressed — evidence-based, at scale | The judgment about *what to do next* is still the human's |
| **MEDDPICC / BANT scoring** | Rep fills fields manually, often optimistically | AI auto-scores fields from call transcripts + CRM activity as a **draft** | The scoring rubric and the 0-3 / 4-4 scales are unchanged |
| **Champion testing** | Rep asserts a champion exists | AI flags whether champion power / influence / personal-win signals appear in recorded interactions | The three-quality champion test is unchanged |
| **Discovery quality** | Subjective review of call notes | AI surfaces missing SPIN question types and un-quantified Implication gaps | SPIN progression is unchanged |

### The Evidence-Based Coaching Loop

```
AI extracts deal evidence (transcripts, attendees, activity)
  -> AI pre-scores MEDDPICC/BANT fields as a DRAFT
    -> Rep validates, corrects, and OWNS the score
      -> Manager coaches on the gap between AI-observed behavior and the framework
        -> Next-action plan is set by the human, informed by evidence
```

The 2026 driver: Salesforce's State of Sales (Feb 2026) reports reps spend only ~28-30% of their week actually selling. AI deal-execution tooling targets that gap by removing manual evidence-gathering and field-filling — but the time it returns is meant to be reinvested in *human* selling and judgment, not in trusting the machine's verdict.

### V2V Caveat (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

**The rep owns the qualification judgment. The AI score is a triage input, never the qualification verdict.** An AI-auto-scored "Economic Buyer: 3 — Validated" is a *draft hypothesis* the rep must confirm against reality, not a fact. Treat AI-extracted MEDDPICC fields exactly as you would a junior rep's first-pass scoring: useful to accelerate, never authoritative. Pipeline-review stage criteria (see `sales-operations.md`) still gate on human-validated evidence, not AI-generated scores. Over-trusting AI scores reintroduces the inflated-pipeline problem the frameworks exist to prevent.
