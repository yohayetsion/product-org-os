## 5. Per-Jurisdiction Detail — Established Laws

### 5.1 New York City — Local Law 144 (the AEDT Law)

**Statute**: NYC Administrative Code §§ 20-870–20-874 (enacted Dec. 2021; enforcement commenced 2023-07-05). Implementing rules: Department of Consumer and Worker Protection ("DCWP") final rules, 6 RCNY §§ 5-300 et seq.

**Why this one matters most**: NYC LL 144 is the most-established US municipal AI hiring statute and the de facto template that other US jurisdictions are drafting toward. The 2026 enforcement posture has shifted: per the December 2025 NY State Comptroller audit findings (publicly released findings reporting NYC employer non-compliance well above 80%), DCWP has moved from reactive complaint-driven enforcement to a proactive posture. V2V operators should assume enforcement attention on NYC AEDT through 2026 and beyond.

**Definition of "AEDT" (6 RCNY § 5-300)**: any computational process derived from machine learning, statistical modeling, data analytics, or artificial intelligence that issues a simplified output (score, classification, recommendation) that is used to **substantially assist or replace** discretionary decision-making for employment decisions. The "substantially assist or replace" clause is the operative scope test — narrow algorithmic tooling that merely surfaces a candidate list without scoring/ranking is arguably out; tooling that produces a score, classification, or recommendation that the employer relies upon is in.

**Scope of employer coverage**: covers (i) employers with employees in NYC, AND (ii) employers using AEDTs on candidates whose work would be performed in NYC (regardless of where the employer is headquartered). A remote employer hiring a NYC resident triggers the law.

**Required controls**:

| Control | Statutory Source | What V2V Skill Must Produce |
|---|---|---|
| Independent bias audit | NYC Admin. Code § 20-871(a); 6 RCNY § 5-301 | Annual audit by an independent third party (no financial interest in the AEDT or the employer); calculation of selection rate and impact ratio per protected category; documentation per DCWP rules |
| Public posting of bias audit summary | NYC Admin. Code § 20-871(b); 6 RCNY § 5-302 | Audit summary date + selection rates + impact ratios posted on a publicly-accessible website at least 10 business days before AEDT use |
| Candidate notice | NYC Admin. Code § 20-871(c); 6 RCNY § 5-303 | 10-business-day notice to candidate that AEDT will be used, including (i) job qualifications/characteristics the AEDT will assess, (ii) data type/sources, (iii) data retention policy |
| Alternative-process disclosure | 6 RCNY § 5-304 | Notice of the candidate's right to request an alternative selection process or reasonable accommodation |

**The 8-step bias-audit checklist** (what an "independent bias audit" must include per DCWP final rules):

1. **Selection rate** calculated for each protected category (race/ethnicity per EEOC categories; sex per binary categories per DCWP rules pending updated guidance; intersectional sex × race/ethnicity)
2. **Impact ratio** for each protected category against the most-favored category (the 4/5ths rule from EEOC Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures, 29 CFR § 1607)
3. **Sample size sufficiency** statement — small-sample categories must be noted; categories with insufficient data must be disclosed rather than masked
4. **Historical data scope** — at least 12 months of AEDT use, or shorter if AEDT was deployed for less than 12 months
5. **Auditor independence** — auditor cannot have financial interest in the AEDT vendor or the employer; cannot have worked on the AEDT's development
6. **Documentation** — methodology, data sources, statistical tests, all calculations
7. **Per-job and per-AEDT version** — separate audits per material AEDT version
8. **Public summary** — selection rates + impact ratios + audit date posted publicly; methodology can be summarized but underlying data need not be published

**Common audit failures** observed in DCWP-pursued enforcement: (i) auditor independence failure (vendor's own data-science team performing the "independent" audit); (ii) intersectional category omission (auditing race alone and sex alone but not race × sex); (iii) historical-test-data substitution (auditing the AEDT on its training data rather than on actual deployment data); (iv) public posting failure (audit conducted but not posted, or posted on a non-public intranet); (v) candidate notice timing failure (notice given at the same time as AEDT screening, not 10 business days before).

**Enforcement and penalties**: DCWP enforcement; $500 first violation per candidate per day, $500–$1,500 per subsequent violation per candidate per day. Multi-candidate violations stack. Class-action exposure is not statutory but is increasingly tested under analogous theories.

**V2V skill implications**: any V2V skill scoring or ranking NYC candidates must (i) require the operator to attest that a current bias audit exists and is publicly posted, (ii) generate the candidate-notice template with the four required disclosures, and (iii) flag for the operator the 10-business-day notice window before the AEDT may run.

### 5.2 New York State — broader AI hiring guidance

### 5.3 Texas — TRAIGA (Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act)

**Statute**: Texas Business & Commerce Code Chapter 552 (TRAIGA), effective **2026-01-01**.

**Scope**: regulates "high-risk AI systems" deployed in employment, defined to include hiring, promotion, termination, compensation, and other consequential workplace decisions. TRAIGA distinguishes between **developers** of AI systems and **deployers** (employer-customers); both have obligations, but deployer obligations are the operative ones for V2V employer skills.

**Required controls for deployers**:

| Control | What V2V Skill Must Produce |
|---|---|
| Notice to candidate/employee | Written notice that AI is being used in the employment decision, before or at the time of use |
| Opt-out right | A mechanism for the candidate/employee to request a non-AI alternative decision process |
| Impact assessment | Pre-deployment impact assessment documenting the AI's purpose, data, accuracy, and risk of algorithmic discrimination; updated annually |
| Risk-management program | Documented program for managing AI risk, including incident reporting to the Texas Attorney General |

**Comparison to NYC LL 144**:
- TRAIGA covers more employment decisions (not just hiring/promotion — also termination and compensation)
- TRAIGA does not require an independent bias audit (unlike LL 144) but does require an impact assessment
- TRAIGA has a statutory opt-out right (LL 144 has an alternative-process disclosure but no statutory opt-out)
- TRAIGA penalties are higher ($100,000 per violation cap vs. LL 144's $500–$1,500 per violation per day)

**V2V skill implications**: any V2V skill operating on Texas candidates or employees must (i) generate a notice template, (ii) wire in an opt-out workflow, (iii) prompt the operator to maintain an impact assessment, and (iv) flag for the operator the annual impact-assessment update obligation.

### 5.4 Colorado — SB 24-205 REPEALED AND REPLACED by SB 25-189 (ADMT disclosure regime)

**Important correction (verified 2026-06-06)**: The earlier baseline that SB 24-205 was merely "postponed to 2027-01-01 by S.B. 26-189" is **WRONG**. SB 24-205 (the Colorado Artificial Intelligence Act) was **REPEALED AND REPLACED**, not postponed. The operative law is **SB 25-189**, passed 2026-05-12, **signed by the Governor 2026-05-14**, **effective 2027-01-01**. (Bill-number formatting varies across sources — some law-firm alerts render it "SB26-189." Use SB 25-189; the substance is firm.)

**What SB 25-189 repeals (from the former SB 24-205)**: it abandons the "high-risk AI system" + algorithmic-discrimination construct entirely and removes the duty of care, the annual impact-assessment requirement, and the risk-management-program requirement. Those obligations no longer exist as Colorado's future state.

**What SB 25-189 replaces them with**: a disclosure-based **"automated decision-making technology (ADMT)"** regime with limited consumer rights — notice of ADMT use, access, correction, and human review on adverse outcomes. Enforcement is **Colorado Attorney General exclusive; no private right of action**. This is a materially lighter regime than the repealed framework — closer to a disclosure/transparency model than to the EU-AI-Act-style risk-management model the original statute contemplated.

**V2V skill implications**: do NOT prompt the operator to comply with the Colorado AI Act in 2026 (no Colorado AI-specific law is in force in 2026). Do NOT carry forward the repealed SB 24-205 risk-management-program / impact-assessment / appeal-rights controls as Colorado's future state. DO prompt the operator to be 2027-01-01-ready for the SB 25-189 ADMT disclosure obligations, and to re-verify against the enacted SB 25-189 text and any implementing rules before any Colorado-affecting output. Cross-reference `hr-ai-governance.md` (Q2-1.B) for the federal Title VII / FCRA frame that applies to Colorado employers in the interim.

### 5.5 California — SB 1162 + ICRAA + CCRAA + pending FEHA AI regulations

**SB 1162 (Pay Transparency)**: in force 2023-01-01. Requires employers with 15+ employees to disclose salary ranges on job postings (including remote postings visible to California candidates). Penalties: $100–$10,000 per violation. Enforcement by the California Labor Commissioner.

**ICRAA (Investigative Consumer Reporting Agencies Act, Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1786 et seq.)** and **CCRAA (Consumer Credit Reporting Agencies Act, Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1785 et seq.)**: California state analogs of federal FCRA, with **additional disclosure and copy-of-report obligations beyond federal FCRA**. Where federal FCRA gives the candidate a copy of the consumer report at adverse action, ICRAA requires the employer to provide a copy of the actual report and to allow inspection at the consumer reporting agency. Private right of action available under both; statutory damages + attorney's fees on top of actual damages. This is the leading FCRA-state-analog regime that V2V skills must enumerate when California is in scope.

**Pending CRD AI regulations under FEHA**: the California Civil Rights Department has proposed regulations under the Fair Employment and Housing Act addressing algorithmic discrimination in employment. Proposed controls include pre-deployment bias testing, retention of bias-test results, and prohibition of facially-neutral AI tools with disparate impact. **Status: pending finalization as of pack version**. V2V operators should monitor CRD for finalization.

**V2V skill implications**: any V2V skill posting jobs visible to California candidates must enforce salary-range disclosure (SB 1162). Any V2V skill producing AI candidate scores that may be acted upon as the basis for an adverse employment action in California must enumerate both federal FCRA (per `hr-ai-governance.md` §FCRA) **and** California ICRAA + CCRAA obligations. Pre-deployment bias testing should be wired into the V2V skill workflow ahead of CRD-rule finalization.

### 5.6 Illinois — HB 3773 (Public Act 103-0804)

**Statute**: amends the Illinois Human Rights Act effective **2026-01-01**. Adds prohibition on employers' use of AI that has the effect of subjecting employees to discrimination on the basis of protected class, and on the use of zip code as a proxy for protected class in AI hiring tools.

**Required controls**:
- Written notice to employees of AI use in employment decisions
- Prohibition on AI that has discriminatory effect on protected classes
- Specific prohibition on zip code as a proxy variable for protected class

**Enforcement**: Illinois Department of Human Rights; per Ill. Human Rights Act civil penalties + injunctive relief.

**V2V skill implications**: V2V skills operating on Illinois candidates/employees must generate the written-notice template and must check for protected-class proxy variables (zip code is explicit; pack-level guidance recommends also checking ZIP+4, neighborhood, high-school graduation cohort, and other proxies) in the model feature set.

### 5.7 Massachusetts — MGL c.151B + MA AG AI Guidance (July 2024)

**Statute**: MGL c.151B (Massachusetts anti-discrimination statute) applies to algorithmic discrimination in employment. Massachusetts has not yet enacted a standalone AI-in-hiring statute, but the **Massachusetts Attorney General issued AI-in-employment guidance in July 2024** affirming that c.151B applies to algorithmic discrimination, that employers are responsible for the discriminatory effects of AI tools they use, and that employers should conduct bias audits of AI hiring tools.

**Statutory bills**: multiple Massachusetts AI hiring bills have been introduced in recent sessions; status pending.

**V2V skill implications**: V2V skills operating on Massachusetts candidates should follow MA AG guidance (bias audit + AI use documentation) as a baseline; monitor for statutory upgrade.

### 5.8 Minnesota — MCDPA Employment Provisions + Pending AI Hiring Bill

**Statute**: Minnesota Consumer Data Privacy Act (MCDPA), effective **2025-07-31**, includes employment-data-profiling provisions. Right to opt out of profiling in furtherance of decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects (which includes employment decisions); right to access, correct, and delete profile data.

**Standalone AI hiring bill**: tracking pending — multiple bills introduced in recent sessions; consult NCSL MN tracker for current status before assuming statutory coverage beyond MCDPA.

## 7. FCRA Crosswalk — State Analogs Layered on Top of Federal FCRA

Per `sensitive-skill-guardrails.md` §1.4, federal FCRA imposes four procedural obligations on adverse employment actions based on AI candidate scoring (pre-adverse-action notice, copy of consumer report, summary of rights, 30-day dispute window + reinvestigation). Several US states have enacted analogs that layer additional obligations on top. The table below identifies the leading state analogs; V2V skills operating in these states MUST enumerate the state analog alongside the federal FCRA enumeration.

| State | State FCRA Analog | Additional Obligations Beyond Federal FCRA |
|---|---|---|
| **California** | ICRAA (Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1786 et seq.) + CCRAA (Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1785 et seq.) | Copy of actual report (not summary); inspection right at the CRA; private right of action with statutory damages + attorney's fees |
| **New York** | NY Fair Credit Reporting Act (NY Gen. Bus. Law Art. 25) | NY-specific disclosure form; expanded permissible-purpose limitations on employment use |
| **Massachusetts** | MGL c.93 §§ 50–68 (MA consumer reporting statute) | Additional pre-employment-screening disclosures; restrictions on certain conviction information |
| **Illinois** | Illinois Personnel Record Review Act (820 ILCS 40) — adjacent regime, not a direct FCRA analog | Right of employees to access and copy their personnel records, including AI-generated employment evaluations |
| **Connecticut, New Jersey, Maine, Vermont, Washington** | Various state consumer-reporting and ban-the-box statutes (non-exhaustive) | Per-state — V2V operator must consult local counsel |

**V2V skill enumeration requirement**: when an AI candidate-scoring skill is operated on a candidate in any of the states above, the skill's `## Cannot Assess Without` section MUST list both the federal FCRA four-obligation block AND the relevant state analog. California is the most consequential because of the private right of action and statutory damages; California-scoped V2V skills should treat ICRAA + CCRAA compliance as a hard requirement.

## 8. Multi-State Compliance Pattern — Strictest-Jurisdiction-Wins

V2V skills operated across multiple US states must comply with the **strictest applicable jurisdiction** for each control. The pattern below is the operational default; operators may relax it only on counsel guidance.

| Control Dimension | Strictest US Standard (as of pack version) | Recommended V2V Default |
|---|---|---|
| Bias audit | NYC LL 144 (independent + annual + public summary + intersectional categories) | All V2V skills run the LL 144 audit pattern, regardless of jurisdiction |
| Candidate notice | NYC LL 144 (10 business days before AEDT use) + Texas TRAIGA (notice at or before use) | 10-business-day notice on all candidates |
| Opt-out right | Texas TRAIGA (statutory opt-out) + Minnesota MCDPA (profiling opt-out) | Wire opt-out workflow on all V2V hiring skills |
| Pay-range disclosure | California SB 1162 (15+ employees, all postings visible to CA candidates) | Disclose pay range on all V2V-generated job postings |
| FCRA enumeration | Federal FCRA + California ICRAA + CCRAA (most stringent state analog) | List federal four-obligation block + California analog in every adverse-action-supporting skill output |
| Protected-class proxy check | Illinois HB 3773 (zip-code prohibition) | Run proxy-variable audit on all V2V hiring-tool feature sets |
| Impact assessment | Texas TRAIGA (deployer impact assessment + annual update). NOTE: Colorado's impact-assessment obligation was REPEALED with SB 24-205 — SB 25-189's ADMT regime does not require it, so this default now rests on Texas TRAIGA alone | Maintain impact-assessment artifact for all V2V hiring skills (driven by Texas TRAIGA) |
| Data-subject rights | Minnesota MCDPA + California ICRAA (inspection right) | Wire data-access + correction + deletion workflows for all V2V hiring-tool candidate data |

Cross-reference to `hr-ai-governance.md` (Q2-1.B): the federal frame (FCRA federal block, Title VII, EEOC Uniform Guidelines, ADA) applies on top of every state layer. Skills must enumerate BOTH the federal frame and the relevant state controls; the state layer never replaces the federal layer.

## 9. Tracking System for Pending State Legislation

State AI hiring law is moving fast. V2V operators should not rely solely on this pack's snapshot — they should maintain an active tracking process. Recommended sources:

- **Trigger-driven**: any time a state AG, DOL, or CRD issues new guidance touching AI in employment, refresh the relevant per-jurisdiction section

## ## Findings (this pack's own application of sensitive-skill scaffolding)

1. **Colorado SB 24-205 was REPEALED AND REPLACED by SB 25-189 (not postponed); no Colorado AI law is in force in 2026** — this is the highest-frequency-confusion point for V2V operators consulting the original 2024 statute text. SB 25-189 (signed 2026-05-14, effective 2027-01-01) replaces the repealed "high-risk AI" framework with an ADMT disclosure regime. The repeal-and-replace correction (NOT "postponement") must be surfaced explicitly in every downstream skill operating on Colorado candidates, and the old SB 24-205 risk-management/impact-assessment obligations must not be carried forward. **Severity: P0.** Next step: any V2V skill that contains a 2026 Colorado compliance prompt — or the prior "postponed via S.B. 26-189" framing — should be patched to reflect the repeal-and-replace to SB 25-189's ADMT model.

2. **NYC LL 144 enforcement posture shifted in late 2025 / early 2026** — the December 2025 Comptroller audit findings (publicly released) raised the political-priority salience of LL 144 enforcement. V2V operators should not assume historical non-enforcement carries forward. **Severity: P1.** Next step: refresh NYC LL 144 operator-facing notice language to reflect the proactive-enforcement environment.

3. **Texas TRAIGA effective 2026-01-01 is live** — V2V skills operating on Texas candidates as of pack publication date must already have notice + opt-out + impact-assessment infrastructure in place. **Severity: P0.** Next step: validate Texas-operating V2V skills against TRAIGA controls before next deployment cycle.

4. **California ICRAA + CCRAA private right of action** — this is the most consequential state FCRA analog because of statutory damages + attorney's fees + class-action potential. V2V skills operating on California candidates that produce AI scoring outputs MUST enumerate ICRAA + CCRAA alongside federal FCRA. **Severity: P0.** Next step: cross-check `/resume-summarizer` and recruiter-workflow skill outputs for California-scoped FCRA enumeration completeness.

5. **Oklahoma and Washington statutory coverage is uncertain** — bills introduced in recent sessions; status pending. V2V operators should not assume statutory coverage beyond federal Title VII / FCRA in these states until NCSL tracker confirms in-force status. **Severity: P2.** Next step: quarterly NCSL refresh; trigger-driven update when bills move.

6. **Illinois HB 3773 zip-code-proxy prohibition is unusually specific** — most state statutes prohibit "discriminatory effect" without enumerating proxy variables; Illinois is explicit. V2V skills operating on Illinois candidates should run a proxy-variable audit that covers zip code AND broader proxy categories (high-school graduation cohort, neighborhood, ZIP+4, etc.). **Severity: P1.** Next step: build a proxy-variable audit utility into V2V hiring-skill scaffolding.

7. **Multi-state operators rarely have a single "strictest jurisdiction" answer per dimension** — the §8 matrix attempts a default, but real operator deployment varies. **Severity: P1.** Next step: V2V hiring-skill outputs should prompt the operator to confirm which states they operate in and surface the per-state controls accordingly, rather than assume the §8 defaults.
